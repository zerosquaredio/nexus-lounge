This hub is run by zerosquared.io (Charles Lucas, Oregon, USA). It keeps as little about you as it can, sells nothing, and shows no ads. This notice says what it keeps, why, and for how long.

1. When you sign in

You sign in with your AT Protocol account (such as a Bluesky account). Your account's server tells the hub your DID (your account's permanent ID) and your handle. The hub never sees your password, can't post or read anything in your account, and keeps no access to it afterwards. It sets a cookie, hub_session, to keep you signed in for up to 7 days; only a scrambled copy of it is stored.

Signed in or not, if you choose a view (how the hub's pages look), a second cookie, hub_view, remembers it for a year, on the hub's pages and on its BBSes' pages at their addresses here; it holds only the view's name, and isn't passed on to the BBSes themselves. How you choose to see art in the chat is remembered in your browser alone.

2. In the common space

  • Your profile: your DID, handle, when you first came and last signed in, and whether you're shown in the space (off until you turn it on).
  • Your BBSes: BBSes you belong to report your membership and whether you're on, for members who chose it there (by your DID and your name there, or by your name alone if you have no AT account); the hub keeps the latest report from each BBS, and your answer when you confirm or deny one.
  • Through a BBS: a BBS you're a member of can show you the common space from inside it. It then receives what you would see there (who is shown, and the chat), and, once you have confirmed that BBS on your profile, what you type there is said in the chat as you. A BBS you haven't confirmed can't speak as you.
  • The map: where you are while you have it open. This isn't stored.
  • Your picture: while you're shown as your own account, the map draws you with the picture from your public Bluesky profile. The hub keeps a copy, so others' browsers fetch it from the hub, not from Bluesky; it checks for a new one every few hours, and deletes it when you turn this off on your profile, remove your profile, or haven't been shown for a week.
  • Chat: messages are kept in memory only, never written down, and are gone when the chat server restarts.
  • Mutes: the people you've muted, so you don't see them.
  • Reports: a report you make about someone keeps your DID, your note, and what was said in the chat in the minutes before it. When the admin closes it, what was said is deleted.

3. If you run a BBS here

Your DID, your BBS's name and its own AT account if you give one, the ports your agent uses, a scrambled copy of its token, when its tunnel was last up, and how much traffic it carried each day.

If your BBS publishes live objects (such as the weather in a town), the hub keeps each one's record, its last readings, and what its program keeps between updates, until the object is taken off your BBS's list or the hub stops running it.

4. Abuse reports

A report sent with the report form keeps what it's about, the kind of problem, what you wrote, how to reach you if you said, and your DID if you were signed in. When the report is closed, what you wrote, how to reach you, and who you are are deleted; what it was about and the outcome stay.

The hub also keeps a record of what its admin does to keep it in order (approving or turning down a BBS, suspending, banning or blocking), naming the BBS or account and the reason, for the long term.

5. Traffic and logs

To carry visitors to a BBS, the hub's edge decrypts the connection and passes it down the BBS's tunnel; it doesn't record what's in it, and keeps no log of pages visited. The hub's own programs log what they do (for example, which BBS connected, or who signed in, by DID) and some internet addresses, such as those of refused connections. Logs never contain messages or what reports say, and are kept for 3 months. Internet addresses are also held in memory for a short time to limit how often someone can try to sign in or send reports.

6. What's public

Some things are public by design, on the AT network: the hub's own account publishes whether each BBS's tunnel is up, and BBSes publish their own directory listings and live objects, with what each object is given, such as a place name or coordinates for the weather; the hub shows those objects, and what they read, to everyone. People allowed into the common space, and the BBSes on this hub (which show it to their members), can see who's shown there and what's said in its chat.

7. Usage totals

The hub keeps daily totals, such as how many people signed in and how much traffic each BBS carried. They hold no DIDs and nothing anyone said, and are kept for the long term.

8. Who else sees it

Nobody, except: the company that hosts the hub's server (OVHcloud, in Oregon, USA); your own AT account's server, when you sign in; the BBSes on this hub, which are run by their own sysops, for what section 2 says passes through them and for whatever you do on them; the services that live objects read, such as Open-Meteo for the weather, which the hub asks, from its own address, about the place each object names (Open-Meteo keeps the coordinates it is asked about in its logs for 90 days); and authorities when the law requires it. Nothing is sold or shared for advertising.

9. Where it's kept, and backups

The hub's server is in Oregon, USA. Its database is backed up nightly to the operator's own equipment in Oregon. Backups are kept for up to 12 months, so something deleted from the hub can remain in a backup until that backup is deleted; backups are used only to restore the hub.

10. Your choices

  • Turn being shown in the common space on or off, and your picture on the map, and see what the hub holds about you, on your profile.
  • Remove your profile there: it deletes your profile, your answers about BBSes, your picture, and your sign-ins.
  • Remove your BBS: what the hub holds about it is deleted, its art among it, except its name, held for 90 days, its daily usage totals (§7), and abuse reports and moderation records about it (§4).
  • Ask about anything else, or ask for a copy or deletion of what the hub holds, by writing to abuse@cyberspace.nexus.

If you're in the EU or UK, you have rights to access, correct, delete, and object to the use of your data, and to complain to your data protection authority. The hub uses your data to provide the service you asked for and, for safety and preventing abuse, in its legitimate interests.

11. Children

The hub is not for anyone under 16. If you believe someone under 16 has signed in, tell us and we'll remove what the hub holds about them.
